Straight answers

The questions everyone asks anyway.

"Our data is sensitive."

Good — it should be. The first thing we set, in any workshop or workflow, is ground rules: what never goes into an AI tool (donor records, client information, anything with a name on it) and what safely can. Most of the wins live in work that was never sensitive anyway — drafts, formats, research, summaries.

"We tried ChatGPT and it didn't stick."

A lunch-and-learn isn't a workflow. Watching someone demo AI and having it run on your own tasks are different sports. The First Workflow ends with it running on your actual task — that's the whole design, and it's why you don't pay if it doesn't.

"Is this about replacing people?"

I'm half of a two-person team that stayed two people. AI covered the roles we were never going to get budget for. That's the honest pitch: capacity you can't afford to hire — not the people you already have.

"What tools do we need? Do we need IT involved?"

Whatever you have. Most teams need one general AI subscription — about the cost of a streaming service per person — and zero IT projects. If your situation genuinely calls for more, it'll be in your plan before you spend a dime.

"We don't have the budget."

The free workshop exists for exactly this — get your association or network to book one. And the First Workflow is $500 on purpose: small enough for a professional-development line item, credited toward bigger training later. If I don't think you'll get your money's worth, my reply will say so.
