## Irresistible Offer thoughts

**Date:** 2026-05-13

**QUESTION:**

Hi – I’d like to discuss my offer simple result and guarantee (per the “irresistible offer”). I’m a PR and media management coach. My audience is: High stakes decision makers, founders, entrepreneurs. I love the simple results of “mastery and perfection.” As measured by improvements on the wheel. Why I think this is because: Previous clients have come to me for: to be sought after, to sell to high-end clients who want a luxury experience, or to sell a new offer/pivot. None of them say “mastery and perfection” I have pro bono clients now. I see them as “perfecting;” they are perfecting their positioning. But when I do organic marketing online by writing posts and connect calls, I feel like my offer – mastery and perfection – is too vague. Please coach me to keep this offer and sell myself on it or change the offer. I can’t decide if I just want to sell myself/make the offer right (perfection) OR change the offer to “prefect their positioning”

**ANSWER:**

HI Coach,

You’re asking a really good question here and I think the bigger thing to look at is not just “should I keep the offer or change it?” but what problem are you actually trying to solve underneath that question.

First, well done putting your offer through the irresistible offer protocol. Let’s dig into your model a little because I think there is awareness here that can help you make the decision instead of spinning in indecision trying to find the “right” wording.

What I hear is:

C — Offer: “mastery and perfection”
T — it’s too vague
F — indecisive / uncertain
A — questioning it, reworking it, trying to solve for certainty
R — more confusion about the offer

Then:

C — Offer: “perfect their positioning”
T — they think this is vague / intangible
F — ?
A — wanting to shift the language, what are you not doing ? What do you do when you think its not tangible? how do you solve for this when you think this? 
R — I don’t get clear and tangible less clear myself

What I notice is you say you LOVE “mastery and perfection.” It feels elegant to you. Refined. Elevated. And I think that matters because when we love our work and our messaging we naturally sell it stronger. But loving it and it being clear to your audience are two different things.

And I think that is the part your brain is trying to sort through.

One thing I notice is when you describe your clients and what they come to you for, they are not using the words “mastery and perfection.” They are saying:

“I want to be sought after.”

“I want luxury clients.”

“I want to pivot.”

“I want to elevate how I’m perceived.”

Not because your offer is wrong, but because clients usually buy in the language of their current problem and desired result, not always the language of our philosophy behind the work.

I also think there’s a difference between:

having the wrong offer vs not yet having messaging that translates clearly yet.

Those are very different problems.

Because honestly, “mastery and perfection” sounds valuable but also somewhat intangible unless you anchor it into how that actually shows up in their life or business.  And it’s not the audience’s job to make intangible work tangible.

That’s our job. Especially online it can be harder to perfect the messaging . Stacey coached here about in person less necessary to be impeccable with message and perfect place to test and improve.

People that know you in person may not need perfectly tangible language because they can feel your conviction and understand what you mean. But colder audiences online don’t have access to your energy in the same way, so clarity matters more.

And I wonder if part of what is happening is your brain is trying to solve uncertainty by finding the “perfect” wording instead of being willing to test and refine messaging over and over.

Sometimes we think we need the right message when really we need willingness to say the same thing 100 different ways until we find what lands for our people.

What feedback are you actually getting?

What are people saying back to you?

What posts or conversations make people lean in more?

What language creates connection fastest?

Those questions matter because messaging is data collection.

And I also want to point out something important around belief. You asked:

“Do I sell myself on the offer or change the offer?”

I think belief work matters a lot here because if your belief in the value of your offer changes daily, your messaging will feel inconsistent too. Belief has to become practiced.

You build belief intentionally.

You repeat it.

You collect evidence for it.

You train your brain to hold it longer.

Otherwise every day your brain wakes up renegotiating the value of your work and then your marketing starts sounding uncertain too. But belief work also does not mean ignoring feedback from your audience.

If your people are clearly telling you they resonate more with “positioning” than “mastery,” that is useful information. Not because your original offer is bad, but because clearer language helps people immediately understand the value.

What I would explore is:

Do you actually want to change the offer?

Or do you want to dial in the messaging around the offer?

For example:

What does “mastery and perfection” actually create for them tangibly?

Does it help them:

become more sought after?

get invited to speak?

land premium clients?

increase influence?

be seen as the obvious expert?

elevate perception in their industry?

That is where the tangible value lives.

Clients often don’t buy “mastery.”

They buy what mastery gets them.

And I think this question could help bring clarity:

If you fully believed your current offer was working and clients were buying easily, would you still want to change the offer? Or would you just refine how you communicate it?

And lastly, I would play with this:

How would you describe what your clients are achieving in 20 different ways to founders and entrepreneurs in your niche? Their language.

Because somewhere in there is probably the bridge between the elegance you love and the clarity your audience needs. Great work Coach!
