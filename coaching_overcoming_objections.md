# Coaching Q&A: Overcoming Objections

## I need to see if I can afford it

**QUESTION:**

People say in my consults – I need to see if I can afford it – do the numbers.


And it seems like that might be a place I could dig in with them – and unearth some objections – but I’ve struggled to know what to say.


I say something like, ‘oh what do you need to look at’


And they say, ‘oh if I can afford it.’


And then I dont know where to go after that.


Should I close the conversation there – or is there something I could say to open up more thinking?

**ANSWER:**

Yes – you want to open up the conversation if they are open to doing that.

How you do that is by you getting curious. You want to get to know them and their mind and how they are thinking and feeling.  It’s not about getting a yes – it’s about creating that safe space for them to really think through this decision. They signed up for the consult with you for a reason.

When you are asking “Oh what do you need to look at?” now, what are you thinking and feeling in that moment? We want to check in to see if there is some thoughts or feelings coming up that are blocking you from knowing what to say next.

When they say “Oh if I can afford it.” – you can get curious about that. You don’t need to follow a script here, but think about what you might ask me if you were coaching me on deciding if I should sign up for a gym membership or not. What might you ask me if I said I wasn’t sure if I could afford it?

It could be something like this:

“Yah, you don’t want to sign up for the gym membership if it’s going to cause more stress due to finances. Do you think you could afford it and there are maybe some other hesitations coming up about going? Or is it really just the money?”

Or maybe “You know you keep thinking about signing up for a gym membership. What do you think would happen if you did decide to do it and spend $___/month? and then, What do you think would happen 3 months from now if you didn’t do it?”

The goal is to create a safe, open space for them to explore with you. Let’s put this into practice now. If you had to re-do the last consult where this came up, what might you have said or asked?

And – are you setting up a follow up call? If someone truly does need to look at their budget, etc, that’s okay. You can set up a follow up call with them so there are clear next steps for them to take. That gives them a chance to do whatever they need to do and still circle back with you so they can ask any questions they might have or have support from you in figuring out their final decision.

## Regular money objection

**QUESTION:**

Hi! I submitted a sales call evaluation just now, and also wanted to get some coaching on an objection I’ve gotten on my last two sales calls. People saying they don’t have the money, and sometimes being willing to make a plan for getting the money, but wanting time to do that.


I know sometimes that the objections we get can be traced back to something going on with us, so I wanted to get some coaching on this to see if there’s something else going on with me that I’m not spotting. The objection under the money objection has been (I think): not believing in herself; uncomfortable spending money on herself; not wanting to ask others for help with money. And the area that I’m working on when it comes to these objections is figuring out how to view these potential clients in a more empowering way, as people who can solve their money challenges, whose dream of writing a novel is worth it.


– What could I ask myself to build my belief around these potential clients?

– What could I ask myself to probe deeper into whether these objections/hesitations are actually coming from me?

– What could I ask myself to dig deeper into why it’s worth investing money to write a novel? I can get stuck here because there isn’t an immediate monetary return with finishing writing a book, but I also know that my life has completely changed because I was able to finish that first book 5 years ago, after decades of unsuccessful attempts. But I think I’m not convicted enough around it?


Appreciate any coaching or perspective you can offer!

**ANSWER:**

Hi Coach,

You’re asking really important questions here, and I actually think the biggest thing I notice is that you’re already aware this may not just be about “handling objections,” but about how you’re relating to the objection when it shows up.

I want to point you first toward the Objections module and the Client/Coach modules because there’s such a powerful way Stacey teaches seeing is often showing you the exact thought loop the client is trapped in. So when someone says, “I don’t have the money,” instead of hearing it as the end of the conversation, you can begin hearing it as the doorway into understanding them more deeply.

What are they actually believing in that moment?

What feeling is driving the hesitation? Because “I don’t have the money” can sometimes mean, “I don’t trust myself.” “What if I fail again?” “What if I spend this and still don’t become who I want to become?” “I don’t know if I’m worth this level of investment.”

That’s very different than simply “I can’t.”

And I think you’re onto something important when you said you’re trying to view your clients in a more empowering way. That matters because clients can feel when we subtly agree with their limitation. Not consciously but energetically, emotionally, in the way we respond, the way we pull back, the way we stop exploring.

One thing I would ask yourself is:

When they give me the objection, do I internally believe it sounds reasonable?

Because if part of your brain goes, “Yeah… I get it… this is a lot of money and maybe it won’t work for them,” you’ll naturally soften, retreat, or stop leading. You may unintentionally join their fear instead of helping them examine it. And this is where sales gets really nuanced.

If exploring objections feels manipulative  you’ll hesitate to fully coach through them. But if you truly believe the coaching could help change the trajectory of their life, then exploring the objection becomes an act of service, not pressure.

That’s why I love the questions you already came up with for yourself, Especially: Why is it worth investing money to write a novel? Sit with that deeply. Flush that out and come up with a robust list of reasons. Spend time ITC on that one so you feel the energy of the value that would create.

Who does someone become by finishing something they’ve spent decades believing they couldn’t do?

What identity shifts?

What self-trust gets built?

What discipline, courage, emotional resilience, or self-respect gets created in the process?

What are the byproducts of becoming a person who follows through on a dream?

Because often the value is not just “the book.” It’s the transformation that happens while becoming the person capable of writing it.

And when you become sold on the deeper value of the work, objections stop feeling like barriers and start feeling like thoughts to explore compassionately.n You can even practice this outside of consults.

Write down every objection you think someone might give you.

What might they actually be afraid of underneath this?

What belief would they need to shift to move forward?

How would I coach someone through this if I fully believed in their ability to succeed? That’s where your confidence gets built.

And during the consult itself, curiosity will serve you far more than trying to “overcome” anything.

“Are you open to exploring what ‘I don’t have the money’ means for you?”

“If the money were available, would this feel like something you’d want to say yes to?” Why ?

And then you can gently explore:

“What would need to happen for this to feel like the best decision you’ve made for yourself?”

“What do you think you’re really afraid might happen if you invest?” “When have you invested in yourself before and not gotten the value? What happened there?”

And also remember every consult is practice. Sales is much more art than script and science.  You’re learning how to read people, how to stay grounded when discomfort appears, how to stay connected instead of rushing to solve or convince.

The more deeply you believe they are capable of creating the result they want, the less afraid you’ll be of the objection itself.

Because objections are often just thoughts people are having in real time about what’s possible for them.

You got this Coach!

**ANSWER:**

Hi Coach,

Thanks for sharing where you are. Of course it feels scary to share your offer. When our brain is afraid people will say no, or that we may seem too forward or arrogant, it tries to use various methods to protect us. Confusion is one of them. In reality, you aren’t really confused. Sharing our offer can be as simple as meeting people, telling people you’re a coach, and making offers to help them. And also, if you need more clarity beyond that, that is what the 2k for 2k modules are for, right? To help teach any skills we lack. I share all of this just so you can see where the brain uses confusion or fear to stall what we may inherently know how to do.

The work here is actually in allowing yourself to 
feel the feelings and take action
 despite and in spite of it.

Your brain simply wants to avoid the 
potential
 
emotions it may feel if someone says “no” or if someone gives feedback that doesn’t feel pleasant. It helps to remind our brain that it’s normal to feel a wide range of emotions, especially as an entrepreneur.

Take some time to reflect and answers these:

1. What would it take for you to take action, even while you feel afraid? Can you do that? Why or why not?


2. What might you learn by allowing yourself to feel fear but take action anyways? How might doing so strengthen who you are as an entrepreneur?

Take time to answer these questions and then bring back your answers here if you’d like more coaching.
