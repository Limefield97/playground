**Date:** 2026-06-10

**QUESTION:**

**ANSWER:**

Hello Coach,

Welcome from Croatia!

Before we can know whether you’re charging “too little”, I’d want to understand why you’ve concluded that price is what’s causing people to “flake.”

What specifically is happening that makes you think, “The value doesn’t match the price in their mind”?

The reason I’m asking is because our brains are very good at filling in the blanks
. Someone doesn’t buy, and we immediately create a story about why. But if we’re solving a problem, we want to make sure we’re solving the actual problem not the one we’ve imagined.

Right now, here are the facts:

You offer 3 months of 1:1 coaching for burnout and stress for women in corporate.

You charge 1,000 EUR.

You’ve enrolled 15 clients and generated 15,000 EUR in the last 9 months.

So if 1,000 EUR is automatically signaling “low value,” how did 15 people decide to work with you at that price?

That’s why I’d be curious to explore what has changed.

When you first started selling this offer at 1,000 EUR, what were you thinking about it? What are you thinking about it now?

Sometimes the offer hasn’t changed. The price hasn’t changed. But the coach’s relationship with the price has changed. All normal it’s going to change that is good we can change the way we think on purpose about the price.

You shared that you already believe 2,000 EUR is a more appropriate price for the work you do. That stood out to me.

When you think, “This should really be 2,000 EUR, but I’m selling it for 1,000 EUR,” how do you feel?

Do you feel fully convicted?

Do you feel generous?

Do you feel resentful?

Do you feel like you’re underselling yourself?

Because how we feel about our pricing often shows up in how we present it. Clients can feel our certainty just as much as they can feel our doubt.

It’s a little like selling a house. The house may be exactly the same, but if the seller keeps apologizing for the price, buyers start wondering what they’re missing.

I’m also curious why you’re waiting until you’re full to raise your rates.

Do you genuinely like that reason?

Or are you borrowing someone else’s pricing strategy while your own thinking has already evolved?

There’s nothing wrong with following the guidance Stacey offers of raising prices once you’re fully booked. That strategy works for many coaches. But it only works when it aligns with your beliefs.

If part of you already believes the offer is worth 2,000 EUR, and another part of you is telling yourself you aren’t allowed to charge that yet, there may be some internal friction worth looking at.

I’d also challenge the assumption that people are not converting because they think the price is too low. 
Have you asked people why they decided not to move forward?

What evidence do you have that  pricing is the reason?

If price wasn’t the explanation, what else might be?

Maybe they aren’t convinced they need help yet.

Maybe they aren’t prioritizing the problem.

Maybe they’re afraid of change.

Maybe your sales process has shifted.

Maybe your messaging has shifted.

Maybe nothing has changed at all, and you’re simply noticing normal sales variability through a different lens.

The goal isn’t to convince yourself the price is right or wrong. The goal is to understand what’s actually happening. 
And then decide intentionally.

What are your thoughts about raising your price now?

What reasons do you have for doing it?

What reasons do you have for not doing it?

Most importantly, do you like those reasons?

Bring your answers back here to Ask a Coach, and we can coach more deeply on what’s underneath this.
