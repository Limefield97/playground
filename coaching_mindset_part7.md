## Building up belief

**Date:** 2019-10-28  
**Topics:** archive-2019-2020

**QUESTION:**

I’m struggling around my belief that I can bring people into my live event. I’m going to make a high-ticket offer (that I’m working on right now), but now all the thoughts are kicking my butt–what if I can’t fill the room? I’ve only sold 20% of the tickets? What if no one signs up for my offer? Oh, Lawdy!!!!

My ideal outcome would be to bring 700 people to the event and sell 20% of the room into my high ticket offer.

**ANSWER:**

First of all, “Oh, Lawdy” is just AMAZING! I love it!

This is a perfect example of cognitive dissonance and how our primitive brains like to freak out whenever we try to achieve something new.  Our brains don’t like change.  They want to keep us safe.  To do this, they want to maintain the status quo.  Any change to the status quo is scary and dangerous and will likely be the death of us (so our brains believe).  And so our brains freak out when we start to think any new thoughts that conflict with our old thoughts.  Any time we try to grow, change, or accomplish something new, we start thinking conflicting thoughts simultaneously.  Right now, you’re conflicting Ts are: “I can create and high-ticket live event and people will buy tickets” and “People won’t buy tickets.”  Whenever you’re thinking two conflicting thoughts, you’re going to experience discomfort.  The truth of the matter is simply this . . . if you want to grow, you have to be willing to feel uncomfortable.  With that said, you can take action despite the negative emotion your feeling.  You can take action deliberately and on purpose.

What if you only sell 20% of the tickets to your first “high-ticket” event?  So what?  Answer that for yourself.  What are you making that mean?  More specifically, what are you making it mean about you?

C: 20% of tickets sold.

T:

F:

A:

R:

What if you chose to focus on what you have instead of what you don’t?  What results would that kind of thinking create?
