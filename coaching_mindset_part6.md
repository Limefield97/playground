**ANSWER:**

Recognize how you’re giving your power away by how your thinking about thoughts.  While you’re recognizing that your thoughts create your feelings, you’re still viewing your thoughts as things that are outside of your control.  Also, you’re also not attributing your thoughts to a particular C.  Don’t skip the C.  There’s always a C, and the C is the basis of every model you run.

T: My thoughts are fleeting.

F:

A:

R:

**Topics:** archive-2019-2020

I want to manage my feelings to be my best NO MATTER WHAT. My brain is seeking validation that I’m NOT worthy of success…and finding it.

**ANSWER:**

It sounds like when you’re doing models, you are able to slide into the second stage of belief– possibility.  Then, the rest of the day, you brain slowly starts to slip back into the first stage of belief–impossibility.  And when that happens, you think something’s gone wrong.  What if that wasn’t the case?  What if it was totally normal?

Brains like to think comfortable thoughts that they’re used to thinking.  New thoughts mean change is on the horizon.  Change is scary, so our brains say.

Instead of just practicing your new thoughts when you’re doing models, practice them all day.  Say them 2k times to yourself.

With that said, what if in addition to practicing those thoughts, you just allowed yourself to feel the negative feelings that you experience when you’re not “believing hard” and still took action anyway?  What do you need to believe about you and the people you want to serve in order to embrace the discomfort and just forge ahead?

## How to allow feelings

**Date:** 2019-10-28  
**Topics:** archive-2019-2020

**QUESTION:**

I’ve seen that my feelings are generating actions and results I don’t want. How do I allow – rather than resist – feelings I don’t want while at the same time creating feelings and thoughts I do want? Sometimes feel stuck here.

I want to know how to allow feelings and also generate new productive ones so I can generate what I want to achieve – more clients.

**ANSWER:**

Learning to allow an emotion is a skill you have to build just like you build a muscle.  You build it through practice.  We resist emotions because, ultimately, we are resisting/avoiding discomfort.  In part because we think we shouldn’t feel negative emotions.  How do we know this isn’t true?  Because we feel them anyways, whether we like it or not.  The funny thing is…by resisting or avoiding emotions we only end up elongating the amount of time we feel them.

Here are a few ways to practice allowing emotions.  First, start by recognizing that emotions are just vibrations in your body.  Think of the absolute worst, most excruciating emotion you can think of.  Maybe it’s shame or humiliation.  Whatever it is, describe how that emotion feels in your body when you feel it.  Get specific.  Write it all down.  What does it feel like in your body?  Where in your body do you feel it?  What makes it different from another negative emotion like heartbreak or sorrow or overwhelm or stress or anger?

Often times, with negative emotions, you’ll will feel your body tighten in some way.  It’s the “resistance” muscle you’ve built up to brace against negative feelings.  Instead of trying to toss away the negative ones like a hot potato, just notice them and breathe them in.  Each feeling is just a vibration.  It can’t actually hurt you.  Practicing allowing negative emotions until you start to recognize that–that they can’t actually harm you.  It isn’t possible.  Whatever the negative emotion, you can handle it.  It will pass.  You’ll be there on the other side of it when it does.  Think of yourself as a train and emotions as passengers.  Passengers get on and get off…the train keeps going.  It doesn’t stop.  Neither will you.
